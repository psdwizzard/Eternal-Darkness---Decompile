## Fresh hypotheses

1. Induction-variable representation: derive the byte offset from `i * 0x18` (directly and through an inner local), relocate increments to the for header, or spell the stride as shifts/addition. MWCC lowers multiplication to a pointer induction variable (`mr r31, r26`, then `addi r31, r31, 0x18`), changing both register use and instructions. It does not produce the retail offset register. Header increments also alter scheduling. Shift/add spelling grows the function to 540 bytes.
2. Parameter coalescing and lexical scope: remove the local `self` alias, combine that with entry-first declaration, or place both induction locals in a nested block. Direct parameter use is neutral; the combinations perturb more preserved registers. This tests joint transformations, not the prior isolated declaration-placement failures.
3. Scalar replacement: group non-escaping pointers or induction variables in a local struct, or represent a local as a one-element array. MWCC scalarizes the aggregates, but the resulting virtual-register order does not match retail. `aggregate-induction` retains 528 bytes but perturbs instruction alignment substantially.
4. Induction type/form: express the entry cursor as an integer address, change the offset to `long`, or maintain its negative and subtract it at the call. Integer-address and long-offset forms are neutral; negative offset changes the recurrence/codegen and regresses.
